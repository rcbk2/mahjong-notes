Faking a Slide

[EN] Opening line: what the tactic is, and that you went to the M.League and Tenhou records to look at it.

7 September 2026EnglishLog analysisDiscard readingTenhouM.League

In short
  • When you draw a 1, 2, 8 or 9, you can discard it from your hand instead, so that it reads as a slide — and your wait looks safe.
  • The spot is rare: once in 708 hands on Tenhou. When it does come, more than one in four is a fake hand discard. In M.League, 12 of 28.
  • The effect splits by shape. Cutting the 1 from 12356 changes nothing; cutting the 2 from 23467 wins by ron +2.9pt more often. Same logic, same lie, different tile — different result.

Faking a slide

[EN] The subject at last: discarding a drawn 1/2/8/9 from the hand instead, so that it reads as a slide.

[EN] The concrete case: holding 12356s and drawing 1s. A draw discard says nothing; a hand discard can look like a slide, given what came before.

The hand — the 1 on the right was just drawn

Discard it honestly — draw discard

What they see: a draw discard. The hand did not move, so nothing is given away.

Discard from the hand — fake hand discard

What they see: a hand discard of 1. It looks like a slide.

↓ Either way the hand left is the same 12356, waiting on 4-7

Actual → waits on

The opponent reasons like this.

1. Read a hand discard of 1 means a slide, so the hand holds 234
2. Suppose＋ if 56 were there too → waits on
3. Contradiction was just discarded by this player. Holding that hand, they would have won long ago
4. Therefore so the is not there. There is no 4-7 wait

So it looks like a slide. What follows? If it really were one, the hand holds 234. Add 56 to that and you get 23456, which waits on 1-4-7. But the 1 has just been discarded by this very player — a hand like that would have won long ago. It has not won, so the 56 is not there. Which is to say: the 4-7 side wait is impossible.

The same logic runs through the mirror image — 45789 drawing a 9 — and through 23467 drawing a 2 (equivalently 34678 drawing an 8). As a rule:

Ready on 12356 waiting 4-7 and you draw the 1; on 45789 waiting 3-6 and you draw the 9; on 23467 waiting 5-8 and you draw the 2; on 34678 waiting 2-5 and you draw the 8 — fake the hand discard.

How often does it happen

Every position where a player was ready in one of these shapes and drew that 1 (9) or 2 (8). Hands that had already declared riichi are excluded: after the declaration there is no choice of discard, so there is no lie to tell.

The two shapes draw karagiri at almost the same rate. On Tenhou more than one in four was a fake hand discard. A dama hand, with everything still concealed, does it more often (33.1%); an open hand, already showing, less (25.5%).

The chance itself is rare

Reaching the shape and then drawing that 1 (9) or 2 (8) at all. Long before the choice comes up, the chance itself is rare — and two entirely separate populations land on almost the same frequency.

Does it work

The point of the lie is to make the 4-7 suji (for 12356) or the 5-8 suji (for 23467) look safe, so that somebody pushes it. Only a player who still has a choice of discard can be fooled, which means one who has not declared riichi. So: did the hand win by ron on that suji, from a non-riichi opponent — when the discard was faked, and when it was not? 6,748 cases from three years of Tenhou Houou south-game records (4.7M+ hands).

The two right-hand columns differ by 12 cases (0.8% of 1,481): hands whose wait moved after the discard, most often onto a pair already held — 9 of them were holding the winning tile at that moment. Put the other way, 99.2% won on the wait they had when they discarded. The shape at that moment is never compound: the extraction requires exactly 112356 or its kin in that suit, so the wait is always the two target tiles.

[EN] 22.8% vs 21.4% overall; −0.72pt for 12356 (interval crosses 0), +2.89pt for 23467 (it does not). Same logic, different tile, different result.

Stronger players do it more

The share who fake the discard rather than take the draw discard rises monotonically with rank. On its own that could just mean stronger players fake discards everywhere. So the blue line adds a baseline: the same players' rate of faking a discard of a tile of the same rank, across every ready hand where they could have — terminal (1/9) for the 12356 rows, 2/8 for the 23467 rows, weighted by how the two shapes divide. It is the strictest control the data allows, matched on the one thing that matters: a spot where a slide could be feigned. Whiskers are 95% intervals.

Notes

No difference between years

Three years of Tenhou pooled. Year by year: 2023, 255 of 992 (25.7%); 2022, 322 of 1,089 (29.6%); Jan-Aug 2026, 187 of 710 (26.3%). 2022 runs a little high, but not by enough to read anything into, and pooling sharpens the rank and rating breakdowns.

Declaration tiles are separate

In the 64 cases where riichi was declared on this shape, 51 (79.7%) were hand discards. That is not a property of the shape. Counting every discard in Tenhou 2023 where a player discarded a tile of the same kind as the one drawn: 75.6% of riichi declaration tiles are hand discards (8,880 of 11,740), against 8.9% of all other discards (351,904 of 3,938,806). Declaration tiles running eight times higher is a feature of the game at large, not of this lie, so they are excluded from the counts above.
